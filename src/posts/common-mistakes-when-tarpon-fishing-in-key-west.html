Tarpon fishing isn’t exactly the same everywhere you go.

Here in Key West, a big part of the experience is built around clear-water sight fishing and the spring and early-summer migration. You’re often looking for moving fish, getting the boat into position and trying to make the right presentation when an opportunity develops.

That creates its own set of mistakes.

Some happen before anglers ever arrive in Key West. Others happen when a Silver King finally shows itself.

Here are a few worth avoiding.

For the complete overview of our local fishery, trips and prices, visit our tarpon fishing in Key West page.

Mistake #1: Ignoring the Key West Tarpon Migration

If tarpon are the main reason you’re coming to Key West, timing matters.

The peak migration occurs during spring and early summer.

That’s the primary seasonal window around which we plan dedicated trips for anglers interested in migrating Silver Kings.

It doesn’t guarantee a catch. No month or date can do that.

But there’s a difference between planning around the documented migration and assuming tarpon fishing is identical throughout the year.

Mistake #2: Expecting One Magic Tarpon Spot

Key West tarpon fishing isn’t about punching one set of coordinates into the GPS every morning.

Fish move.

Conditions change.

The shallow-water fishery around Key West includes flats, channels and backcountry environments, while the remote Marquesas add crystal-clear flats, channels and mangroves farther west.

Your guide makes decisions based on what the fish and conditions are doing that day.

That’s more useful than knowing where somebody caught one yesterday.

Read more about the local environments in where to fish for tarpon in Key West.

Mistake #3: Not Being Ready to Sight-Cast

When conditions are right, Key West gives us the opportunity to sight-cast to migrating tarpon in clear water.

That means your opportunity can appear quickly.

Your captain sees a fish and begins positioning the boat. Maybe you see a roll. Maybe it’s a tail or flash of silver.

Or maybe you don’t see it at all.

That’s where anglers sometimes lose their opportunity. They keep trying to find the fish themselves instead of listening to the guide and getting ready to cast.

Bring polarized sunglasses and pay attention.

Your captain spends far more time looking at this water than you do.

Mistake #4: Casting At the Tarpon Instead of Presenting to It

When anglers finally see a big tarpon, there’s a natural temptation to throw directly at it.

A better presentation gives the fish a chance to find what you’re offering.

That’s especially important with moving fish.

With a streamer fly, for example, the existing Key West Pro Guides guidance is to lead the fish rather than dropping the fly directly on top of it.

The exact presentation varies, but the basic lesson is the same:

Don’t let the excitement of seeing the fish ruin the opportunity.

Mistake #5: Thinking Key West Tarpon Fishing Means Fly Fishing Only

Fly fishing is a major part of the Key West tarpon experience.

It’s not the only way we do it.

Key West Pro Guides targets tarpon with flies, live bait and plugs. Documented live bait options include mullet and pinfish.

Different opportunities call for different approaches.

If you’ve dreamed about catching a Key West tarpon on fly, tell us. But don’t assume you need to be an experienced fly angler to book a tarpon trip.

Instruction is included.

Mistake #6: Overlooking the Backcountry

Key West isn’t just offshore water.

Our backcountry fishery includes islands, basins, channels, creeks and shallow waters. It’s a scenic, protected side of Key West and an important part of understanding the local shallow-water fishery.

Dedicated Tarpon Trips and standard Backcountry Fishing trips are different offerings, so choose based on what you actually want from the day.

For the broader trip option, see our backcountry fishing in Key West page.

You can also learn how the two fisheries connect in our guide to backcountry tarpon fishing in Key West.

Mistake #7: Treating Peak Migration Like a Guarantee

Spring and early summer are the peak migration.

They’re not a promise.

Fishing conditions change. Fish move. Some opportunities work out and others don’t.

Key West Pro Guides doesn’t guarantee catches.

What our full-time professional captains provide is local knowledge, instruction, equipment and a day built around making the most of the conditions you’re given.

That’s an important distinction when planning any tarpon trip.
